RandomBench

How well can language models pick numbers at random?

Each model was asked many times for a random integer from 0 to 99, in a fresh conversation every time. A model that gives the same answer every time scores 0.01, and a perfect random number generator scores about 1.

__TICKS__

Answers draws how often the model gave each value, from 0 at the left to 99 at the right, with its favorite in orange. Each bar's height is in proportion to its count, with a floor so that a value given only once still shows.

Replies counts every call that got a reply, valid or not. Calls that failed, such as those the quota refused, aren't counted. Every score's 95% interval comes from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the model's replies, so a model with fewer replies gets a wider interval, not a lower score.

The bias checks on a share of the answers, such as Multiples of 10, are lenient toward avoidance. A model that never answers a multiple of 10 is only 10% away from the ideal share, so it scores 0.900, and one that answers nothing else scores 0.100.

__PANEL__

What stands out

No model comes close. Even the most random model scores less than 0.2, and the top of its interval is less than 0.2 too. A perfect random number generator scores about 1.

Three numbers dominate. Nearly every model's favorite is 42, 47, or 73, and those three numbers make up most of all the answers. The exceptions are gpt-oss-120b, whose favorite is 57, and GPT-5.4, which answers 57 and 47 about equally often. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and Claude Opus 4.6 have answered nothing but 42, and Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 answer 47 about 9 times in 10.

Newer isn't more random. GPT-5.4 ranks first, ahead of every later GPT, from GPT-5.5 to GPT-6.1. Gemini 2.5 Flash, one of the oldest models here, ranks second, well ahead of every newer Gemini. Grok 4.7 is less random than Grok 4.6, GPT-6 Luna than GPT-5.6 Luna, and Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 than Sonnet 4.6. Some go the other way: Claude Haiku 5.5 is more random than Haiku 4.5, and Claude Opus 5.5 is the most random Claude. None of these comparisons has overlapping intervals.

Whole regions go unused. More than 95% of all answers lie between 20 and 79, and more than 95% end in 2, 3, or 7. Only the three most random models, GPT-5.4, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and Grok 4.6, have answered above 89 or with a repeated digit like 77. Only GPT-5.4 and Grok 4.6 have answered below 10, and only GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.6 Terra with a multiple of 10. Every other model has avoided all of these, and multiples of 5 too, so they all get the same score on Multiples of 10, Multiples of 5, Edges, and Repeated Digits. Here the identical scores are the finding.

A share check can pass by accident. gpt-oss-120b scores close to perfect on primes. Its share of primes is near the ideal 25%, but it comes from its three favorites, 57, 42, and 73, of which only 73 is prime. Every model whose favorite is 47 or 73, both prime, answers with a prime more than half the time, twice what chance would give. Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 do more than 95% of the time.

Nearly every reply is valid. The one big exception is Claude Opus 4.8 when it's asked to draw a raffle winner. It declines nearly every time, saying it can't draw a truly random number, and suggests a better way, such as random.org, a spreadsheet's RANDBETWEEN, or Python. A few of those replies offer a casual pick anyway, and some warn that it would favor numbers like 37, 42, or 73. Its other replies are all valid. Grok 4.6 now and then adds a confidence score after its answer, as in 47\n\confidence{80}, and the second number makes the reply invalid. Claude Opus 5.5 has slipped twice, once with an empty reply and once by writing out its reasoning before the number. Every other model's replies have all been valid.

Reasoning helps a little, but doesn't fix it. Ten models reason at length by default, spending a median of more than 50 output tokens on a two-digit answer, and Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4.6 more than 200. The models that spend more tokens tend to score higher, but the most random, GPT-5.4, answers in a median of 4. It isn't a clean comparison, since reasoning goes with provider: all ten are from Google or xAI, or are gpt-oss-120b. And the models that reason are still far from uniform.

The official leaderboards are on Kaggle. RandomBench ranks the models by score, and RandomBench Biases holds the nine checks. They show the same scores without the intervals.
