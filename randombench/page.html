RandomBench

How well can language models pick numbers at random?

Each model was asked many times for a random integer from 0 to 99, in a fresh conversation every time. A model that gives the same answer every time scores 0.01, and a perfect random number generator scores about 1.

Each cell is shaded from its check's lowest possible score, unshaded, up to 1. Uniformity and Last Digit count values, so they're shaded on a log scale: a model that always gives one answer is unshaded, a fair pick among 10 of the 100 values is halfway, and all 100 is full.

The share checks are lenient toward avoidance. A model that never answers a multiple of 10 is only 10% away from the ideal share, so it scores 0.900, and one that answers nothing else scores 0.100. Every score has a 95% interval from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the model's replies.

What stands out

No model comes close. Even the most random model is as repetitive as a fair pick among fewer than a fifth of the 100 values. A perfect random source would use all 100.

Three numbers dominate. Every model's favorite is 42, 47, or 73, except gpt-oss-120b's, which is 57. Those three numbers make up most of all the answers. Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite has answered nothing but 42, and Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 answer 47 about 9 times in 10.

Newer isn't more random. Gemini 2.5 Flash, one of the oldest models here, ranks first, well ahead of every newer Gemini. Grok 4.7 is less random than Grok 4.6, GPT-6 Luna than GPT-5.6 Luna, and Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 than Sonnet 4.6. Claude Haiku 5.5 goes the other way, more random than Haiku 4.5. None of these comparisons has overlapping intervals.

Whole regions go unused. More than 95% of all answers lie between 20 and 79, and more than 95% end in 2, 3, or 7. No model has answered a multiple of 10 or a number below 10. Only Gemini 2.5 Flash has answered above 89 or with a repeated digit like 77, and only it and Grok 4.6 with a multiple of 5. Avoiding a set entirely gives every other model the same score on those four checks. Here the identical scores are the finding.

A share check can pass by accident. gpt-oss-120b scores close to perfect on primes. Its share of primes is near the ideal 25%, but it comes from its three favorites, 57, 42, and 73, of which only 73 is prime. Every model whose favorite is 47 or 73, both prime, answers with a prime more than half the time, twice what chance would give. Claude Sonnet 5 and 5.5 do more than 95% of the time.

Nearly every reply is valid. The only invalid replies so far are Grok 4.6's. Now and then it adds a confidence score after its answer, as in 47\n\confidence{80}, and the second number makes the reply invalid.

Reasoning helps a little, but doesn't fix it. Many of the models reason by default. Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4.6 each spend a median of more than 200 output tokens on a two-digit answer. The models that spend more tokens tend to score higher, and the five least random answer in five tokens or fewer. It isn't a clean comparison, since reasoning goes with provider: every Claude model answers in five tokens or fewer. And the models that reason are still far from uniform.

The official leaderboards are on Kaggle. RandomBench ranks the models by score, and RandomBench Biases holds the nine checks. They show the same scores without the intervals.
